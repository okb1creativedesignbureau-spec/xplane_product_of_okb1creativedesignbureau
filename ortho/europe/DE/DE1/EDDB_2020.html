Freezing Aviation History: Digitizing COVID-Era Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB 2020) in X-Plane 12

How 2020 aerial survey imagery, custom raster calibration, taxiway re-routing, and static ghost fleet placement captured BER's historic opening during the global aviation shutdown.

If you fly into Berlin Brandenburg Airport (EDDB / BER) in modern desktop flight simulators, you typically experience one of two things: a generic modern hub bustling with active gate traffic, or legacy scenery layouts that still struggle to bridge the gap between old Schönefeld (SXF) infrastructure and the central Willy Brandt terminal.

However, 2020 was a year unlike any other in aviation history.

On October 31, 2020 — after nearly a decade of notorious construction delays — BER officially opened its doors. But instead of vibrant international launch events, the airport opened right into the teeth of the global COVID-19 pandemic. Millions of square meters of pristine new tarmac, brand-new taxiway networks, and dual parallel runways were immediately converted into massive storage yards for grounded airline fleets across Europe.

At OKB-1 Creative Design Bureau, we set out to capture this exact frozen moment in time. Using high-resolution 2020 georeferenced raster imagery, custom WED vector calibration, and precise ground routing, our EDDB 2020 Orthophoto Immersive Edition recreates Berlin Brandenburg during its surreal, historic opening year.

1. The Opening Day Lockdown

When Terminal 1 finally received its first commercial arrivals (a synchronized double landing by Lufthansa and EasyJet), global air traffic had collapsed by over 70%. BER did not open as a bustling international hub; it opened as a quiet fortress. Aircraft from major European carriers were lined up nose-to-tail on newly constructed taxiways and remote aprons that had never seen a revenue passenger.

2. The Taxiway Re-Routing Challenge

In 2020, several taxiways surrounding the south runway (07R/25L) were either undergoing final operational adjustments or actively blocked off for static aircraft parking. Flight simulators operating on modern 2024–2026 navdata force AI and ATC routing through paths that were physically obstructed during the 2020 freeze. Restoring EDDB 2020 required re-aligning and re-routing ground navigation lines to match the exact 2020 operational ground layout.

Creating an authentic orthophoto package means going far beyond simply dropping an aerial image onto the terrain mesh. It requires sub-meter spatial accuracy, surface calibration, and hand-placed historical assets.

  1. Gateway Baseline Integration & Raster Calibration: The scenery is built upon the official Scenery Gateway layout, inheriting Laminar's core airport infrastructure while completely re-calibrated against high-resolution aerial survey data captured during the 2020 survey period by GeoBasis-DE / LGB. Every inch of the twin runways, high-speed turnoffs, central apron, and surrounding logistics zones matches the real-world surface conditions of Berlin Brandenburg during its opening months.
  2. Parked Fleet & Ground Markings Integration: Captured directly within the orthophoto underlay are the actual rows and lines of aircraft parked across BER's tarmac during the COVID shutdown. Combined with calibrated ground painted lines, lead-in taxi guidelines, and updated stop bars, the airfield projects an unmistakable visual weight and atmosphere upon arrival.
  3. Re-Calibrated Ground Navigation & ATC Networks:
    • Runway & Taxiway Alignment: All paved surfaces, hold-short lines, and taxiway designations were individually recalibrated against 2020 aeronautical charts over the Gateway framework.
    • Ground ATC Rerouting: Navigation nodes were rebuilt in WorldEditor (WED) to prevent simulated ground traffic from routing through closed tarmac zones or parked aircraft lines.
  4. Zero Dependencies: Simple Drag-and-Drop Installation: We designed this scenery with thermal efficiency and usability as top priorities. You do not need to hunt down dozens of external third-party object libraries or deal with complex installation tools. It is 100% drag-and-drop ready, relying entirely on native X-Plane 12 surface rendering and custom raster layering.

⚙️ Installation & Setup Guide

To ensure X-Plane 12 loads your restored EDDB 2020 layout cleanly over default global assets, follow this simple folder hierarchy:

  1. Download and extract the project archive: EDDB - Berlin Brandenburg 2020 Ortho.
  2. Move the extracted folder directly into your X-Plane 12 Custom Scenery directory: 
    X-Plane 12/Custom Scenery/EDDB - Berlin Brandenburg 2020 Ortho/
  3. Launch X-Plane 12 once to automatically update your scenery_packs.ini file, then exit the simulator.
  4. Open Custom Scenery/scenery_packs.ini in any standard text editor and ensure the EDDB entry sits ABOVE Global Scenery/Global Airports/: 
    SCENERY_PACK Custom Scenery/EDDB - Berlin Brandenburg 2020 Ortho/
...
SCENERY_PACK Global Scenery/Global Airports/
  5. Launch X-Plane 12, select EDDB, set your time and weather, and enjoy flying into 2020 Berlin Brandenburg!

💡 Developer & WED Editing Note:
If you open this package inside WorldEditor to adjust ground flows or custom gate starts, always export using File -> Export Scenery Pack. To clear cached DSF geometry in-sim without restarting X-Plane 12, press Ctrl + R to reload scenery.
