The Lost Wings of Belarus: Digitally Restoring Minsk-1 Airport (UMMM) in X-Plane 12

How the iconic urban airfield that powered Soviet Belarusian aviation for over 80 years was recreated in WED before disappearing under modern real estate.

1. An Aviation Hub Inside the City Center

Located just a few kilometers south of central Minsk, Airport Minsk-1 Click to open side panel for more information (UMMM / MHP) was one of Europe’s true "city airports." Commissioned in 1933, it served as the primary civil aviation hub for Belarus for nearly half a century.

During the Soviet era, Minsk-1 was a bustling node for regional feeder flights, agricultural aviation, and state VIP transfers, connecting the Belarusian SSR with major cities across the USSR. Pilots operating at Minsk-1 faced a unique operational environment: a single 2,000-meter paved runway (12/30) situated right against dense urban neighborhoods, industrial quarters, and apartment blocks.

2. Technical Profile: Minsk-1 (UMMM)

  • ICAO / IATA Designators: UMMM / MHP
  • Geographic Coordinates: 53.8644∘N, 27.5397∘E
  • Elevation: 228 meters / 748 feet AMSL
  • Primary Runway: Runway 12/30 (2,000 m × 60 m / Asphalt & Concrete)
  • Operational Status: Closed (2015) / Demolished & Redeveloped into “Minsk World” (Минск Мир)

As air traffic grew and larger jet transports like the Tu-154 and Il-62 became mainstream, operations shifted to the much larger Minsk-2 (Minsk National Airport, UMMS) in 1982. Minsk-1 remained active for regional flights, general aviation, and Aircraft Repair Plant No. 407 until its official closure on December 23, 2015. Today, the airfield’s physical footprint has been largely cleared for urban redevelopment.

3. Why Recreate Minsk-1 in X-Plane 12?

While default flight simulator scenery replaces historical city airfields with generic green land class or post-demolition satellite imagery, custom WED restoration preserves this crucial piece of aviation history in full 3D:

  • Authentic City Approaches: Experience low VFR/IFR approaches directly over the roofs of central Minsk onto Runway 12/30.
  • Aircraft Repair Plant №407 (ARZ 407): Recreates the famous maintenance factory aprons where classic Soviet airframes like the Tu-134, Yak-40, and An-2 were serviced for decades.
  • Historic Terminal Architecture: Accurately models the Stalinist Empire-style main terminal building constructed in 1957, bringing back the unique mid-century Soviet aesthetic.
  • Perfect Hub for Vintage Soviet Props: Fly authentic regional routes connecting Minsk-1 with regional Belarusian strips, Moscow, Kyiv, or the Baltic states in aircraft like the An-2, An-24, Tu-134, or Yak-40.
