Resurrecting Forgotten Wings: The Historic Old Vladivostok Airfield (RU-0536) in Modern Flight Simulation

How early 20th-century aviation infrastructure in Russia’s Primorsky Krai is being preserved through digital recreation.

1. The Dawn of Aviation in the Russian Far East

Long before the modern jet bridges and concrete expanses of Vladivostok International Airport (Knevichi) dominated the skies of the Far East, aviation in Primorsky Krai was a daring, pioneering endeavor. In the early 1930s — around 1931 to 1932 — the Soviet Union began carving out basic landing strips across the Maritime Province to establish essential transport, military presence, and postal links across vast, rugged terrain.

Key locations such as Vtoraya Rechka and rural strips near Artyom served as crucial early hubs. Flying in the Russian Far East during this era required immense skill, navigating unpredictable coastal weather, dense fog, and basic unpaved grass or gravel runways without modern radio navigation aids.

Among these legacy installations sat the Old Vladivostok Airfield (referenced under location tag RU-0536).

2. Technical Profile: Old Vladivostok Airfield (RU-0536)

Located in what was formerly known as the Soviet Far Eastern aviation network, the airfield sits within the geographic coordinates of Latitude 43.20730, Longitude 131.93870, placing it directly inside the Khabarovsk Flight Information Region (FIR).

  • Location Tag / Reference: RU-0536 (Uncertified / Legacy strip)
  • Region: Primorsky Krai (Maritime Territory), Russian Federation
  • Coordinates: 43°12'26.3"N 131°56'19.3"E (43.20730, 131.93870)
  • FIR Responsibility: Khabarovsk FIR
  • Status: Abandoned / Closed

As Soviet aviation consolidated after World War II and into the Cold War, regional infrastructure shifted toward unified, larger airbases capable of supporting heavy jet transports and military interceptors. Smaller auxiliary strips like RU-0536 were gradually phased out, reclaimed by nature, or swallowed by urban expansion around the expanding Vladivostok metropolitan area.

3. Why Recreate Forgotten Soviet Airfields in Digital Flight Simulation?

While modern flight simulators excel at depicting major international hubs, there is a profound educational and historical value in preserving small, forgotten airfields.

Recreating an airfield like RU-0536 in X-Plane offers a unique window into history:

A. Living Aviation History

Instead of reading about historic airfields in textbooks, flight simmers can physically fly the approaches, experience the terrain constraints, and appreciate the operational challenges faced by early Soviet aviators flying legendary aircraft like the Polikarpov Po-2, Antonov An-2, or Lisunov Li-2.

B. Geographic Realism & Visual Context

By meticulously placing ground textures, custom structures, and accurate runway dimensions into X-Plane using WorldEditor (WED), digital preservationists bridge the gap between historical maps and interactive 3D space.

C. Exploring the Far East VFR Routes

Primorsky Krai offers breathtaking terrain — where coastal mountains meet the Sea of Japan. Flying VFR (Visual Flight Rules) between historic strips provides flight simulation enthusiasts with an authentic alternative to mainstream routes.

4. Re-Living the Era in X-Plane

This historical site has been brought back to life as a detailed digital recreation inside the X-Plane flight simulation engine. Pilots can now fire up their engines, navigate the Khabarovsk FIR, and experience a forgotten piece of Maritime Territory aviation history firsthand.

Whether you are an aviation history enthusiast, a pilot looking for challenging Far-Eastern VFR routes, or a scenery collector, digital historic recreations keep these lost airfields alive for generations to come.
