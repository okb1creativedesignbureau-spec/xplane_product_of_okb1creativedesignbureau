Unbundling Knevichi: The Evolution of Lake Springs Heliport (Ozerne Klyuchi)

How an auxiliary military and general aviation apron evolved into an independent rotary-wing airfield in Primorsky Krai — and its restoration in X-Plane 12.

1. From Integrated Military Complex to Standalone Airfield

Historically, aviation infrastructure in Russia’s Maritime Territory (Primorsky Krai) was designed around consolidated military-industrial clusters rather than isolated, single-use airfields. Lake Springs (historically known locally as Ozerne Klyuchi) originated as an integral auxiliary sector of the broader Vladivostok / Knevichi (UHWW) air transport and defense zone.

During the Cold War, the Lake Springs sector performed several vital roles under the umbrella of the Knevichi airbase network:

  • Dispersal & Secondary Operations: Serving as a dedicated rotary-wing and light utility transport base, keeping low-speed helicopters and general aviation airframes clear of the primary jet fighter aprons and main runways at Knevichi.
  • Search and Rescue (SAR) & Patrol: Housing Soviet Naval Aviation (AV-MF) and V-PVO utility detachments responsible for coastal maritime surveillance, SAR, and local logistics across the Sea of Japan coastline.
  • Civilian Regional Auxiliary: Supporting DOSAAF light aviation, regional aerial work, and medical evacuation (Sanavia) flights servicing remote settlements throughout Primorsky Krai.

As military posture shifted and civilian airspace was reorganized post-1990s, the operational links between the main civilian international terminal, the northern fighter aprons, and the southern rotary-wing sectors were re-drawn. Lake Springs transitioned from an internal auxiliary apron of the Knevichi complex into a distinct, standalone airfield facility catering to general aviation, civil utility, and helicopter operations.

2. Technical Profile: Lake Springs Airfield / Heliport

While sharing the surrounding airspace with the primary instrument arrival routes for UHWW, Lake Springs functions as a low-altitude VFR hub inside the Khabarovsk FIR:

  • Location Reference: Lake Springs / Ozerne Klyuchi (Adjacent to UHWW / Knevichi)
  • Geographic Position: Approx. 43.38 N, 132.16 E
  • FIR Responsibility: Khabarovsk FIR
  • Primary Airframe Types: Rotary-wing aircraft (Mi-8, Mi-2, Ka-27/Ka-32 variants) and light utility fixed-wing planes (An-2, general aviation types).
  • Operational Environment: Low-altitude VFR corridors, specialized heliport pads, hard-surface light runways, and localized maintenance hangars.

3. The Operational Dynamic in Flight Simulation

In default X-Plane 12 scenery, satellite landclass and automated airport generation algorithms treat Lake Springs as either an unused grass field or absorb it seamlessly into the generic terrain around UHWW. This ignores the distinct operational value of having a dedicated rotary-wing and general aviation hub right next to a major international airport.

Restoring Lake Springs as a standalone facility in WorldEditor (WED) introduces several unique flight simulation scenarios:

  1. Integrated Airspace Coordination: Operating rotary-wing VFR procedures out of Lake Springs requires careful altitude and corridor discipline to avoid interfering with heavy commercial jets flying the ILS Category II approach into Runway 25L at Knevichi.
  2. Search & Rescue / Utility Missions: Serves as the perfect staging ground for Mi-8 or Ka-32 operations — flying offshore maritime SAR missions over the Amur Bay, forestry patrols, or sling-load operations into the Sikhote-Alin mountain range.
  3. Historical Realism: Reconstructs the original hardpoints, service aprons, and local hangars that defined the site during its dual military/civil auxiliary era.
