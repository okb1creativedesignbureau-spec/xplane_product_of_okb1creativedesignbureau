Reconstructing Johannisthal-Adlershof (1928): Digitizing the Birthplace of German Aviation in X-Plane 12

How 1928 aerial survey imagery, historic 1916 manufacturer hangar georeferencing, and multi-directional runway modeling brought Germany’s legendary Motorflugplatz back to life.

Long before Berlin Tegel (EDDT) or Tempelhof (EDDI) dominated the skies of Central Europe, a sprawling field southeast of Berlin served as the epicentrum of early powered flight: Motorflugplatz Johannisthal-Adlershof. Opened in 1909 as Germany’s first commercial airfield, Johannisthal was not merely an landing strip — it was the industrial engine and research cradle of early 20th-century aviation.

In modern desktop flight simulation, Johannisthal is either absent or rendered as a reducedflatland (which I made it as modern General Aviation / glider strip here). At OKB-1 Creative Design Bureau, we reconstructed the field as it stood during its operational golden era in 1928, restoring its multi-directional runway layout and historic aerospace manufacturing compounds directly from archival aerial survey photography.

1. Georeferencing the Baseline: 1916 Aero-Manufacturer Anchors

To achieve sub-meter historical alignment across X-Plane 12’s terrain mesh, we anchored our WorldEditor (WED) spatial reference framework directly to the preserved architectural footprints of 1916 Imperial German aero-engine and airframe factories.

2. Multi-Directional Grass Runway System

Before the era of single-direction paved tarmac strips, major European airfields operated open multi-directional grass landing zones to ensure aircraft could always launch and land directly into the prevailing wind.

While X-Plane 12’s ATC logic requires defined runway vectors, we mapped four primary operational runway vectors intersecting across the central airfield polygon.

✈️ Operational Capability:
Unlike modern grass glider strips, Johannisthal in 1928 featured usable landing vectors exceeding 1.0 km in length. This gives simulator pilots ample ground roll distance to land heavy interwar airliners (Junkers G.24, F.13, Ford Trimotor), vintage warbirds, or even modern gravel-rated jets like the VFW-614 or Boeing 737–200 Gravel Kit!

3. Industrial Infrastructure & Research Compounds

Surrounding the landing field, we populated the perimeter with detailed 3D representations of historic research facilities and engine manufacturing plants, including the hangar location early facilities of the Deutsche Versuchsanstalt für Luftfahrt (DVL) — the direct predecessor to modern German aerospace research centers (DLR).

  • Calibrated PBR Surface Materials: Custom grass and compacted earth polygons designed to reflect dynamic wetness and lighting under X-Plane 12’s physical atmosphere.
  • Historical Accuracy: Stripped away all modern 21st-century urban encroachment, restoring the expansive open perimeter boundaries of the late Weimar Republic era.
