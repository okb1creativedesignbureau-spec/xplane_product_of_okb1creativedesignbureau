Resurrecting RAF Gatow (1953): Digitizing Berlin’s Cold War Air Corridor Gateway in X-Plane 12

📅 Published: November 8, 2026 • 📁 Category: Germany Lost Airport

During the heighten tension of the Cold War and the historic Berlin Airlift of 1948–1949, RAF Gatow served as a critical lifeline connecting Western Allied forces to British-controlled Sector Berlin. Positioned on the banks of the Havel River in western Berlin, Gatow handled thousands of tons of coal, foodstuffs, and essential supplies daily alongside Tempelhof and Tegel.

Historic Timeline: RAF Gatow

+-------------------------------------------------------+
|              HISTORIC TIMELINE: RAF GATOW             |
+-------------------------------------------------------+
| 1935: Constructed as a Luftwaffe flight academy.      |
| 1945: Captured and turned over to Royal Air Force.    |
| 1948: Essential hub for the Berlin Airlift.           |
| 1953: Modernized runways & radar approach nodes.       |
| 1994: RAF drawdown; converted to Military Museum.     |
+-------------------------------------------------------+
  • The Cold War Lifeline: RAF Gatow handled thousands of sorties carrying supplies during the 1948–1949 Berlin Airlift operation.
  • Royal Flight Gateway: Gatow acted as the primary entry point for British diplomatic, VIP, and Royal Flight operations into West Berlin throughout the mid-20th century.
  • Heritage Preservation: Today, the airfield remains preserved as the Militärhistorisches Museum der Bundeswehr (Flugplatz Berlin-Gatow).
