Resurrecting RAF Gatow (1953): Digitizing Berlin’s Cold War Air Corridor Gateway in X-Plane 12

How 1953 aerial orthophotos, legacy 1950s runway markings, active NDB/ILS navigation recovery, and native Laminar asset optimization brought West Berlin’s Allied lifeline back to life.

If you take off in default X-Plane 12 from the southwestern outskirts of Berlin, the area surrounding the historic airfield of RAF Gatow (EDBG) presents a familiar modern reality: a quiet, residential landscape fading into dense eastern tree lines along the River Havel.

This isn’t surprising. RAF Gatow officially closed its military runways in 1994 following the end of the Cold War and the reunification of Germany, eventually turning into the Militärhistorisches Museum der Bundeswehr and modern housing estates. In default flight simulator databases, Gatow is a ghost field.

At OKB-1 Creative Design Bureau, we refuse to let Cold War aviation history remain dormant beneath modern suburban sprawl. As part of our Germany Lost Airport Restoration Project, we have reconstructed RAF Gatow as it stood during its operational zenith in 1953 — rebuilding its airfield infrastructure piece-by-piece from georeferenced 1953 aerial survey imagery, reactivating legacy navigation data, and applying authentic 1950s British military runway marking systems.

🏛️ Deep Dive: The Forgotten History & Airline Trivia of RAF Gatow

Before Berlin Tegel (EDDT) or Tempelhof (EDDI) handled the bulk of West Berlin’s commercial traffic, RAF Gatow was the absolute center of gravity for British sector aviation.

1. The Crucible of the Berlin Airlift (1948–1949)

During Operation Plainfare (the British component of the Berlin Airlift), Gatow set world records for airfield throughput. At its peak, a transport aircraft landed or took off every 90 seconds. Because the airfield originally possessed only a single concrete runway, Royal Engineers laid down a parallel 1,500-meter pseudo-runway made entirely of Pierced Steel Planking (PSP) in under six weeks, alongside a specialized water-landing slipway on the adjacent Lake Groß-Glienicke for Short Sunderland flying boats.

2. British European Airways (BEA) & Commercial Airline Ops

While Tempelhof was the American sector’s hub, Gatow served as the primary commercial airport for the British sector until commercial flights transitioned to Tegel in 1968.

  • BEA’s Berlin Base: British European Airways operated regular passenger routes connecting West Berlin to West Germany (Hamburg, Hanover, Düsseldorf, and Cologne) using Vickers Vikings, Douglas C-47 Dakotas, and later the iconic Vickers Viscount.
  • The “Air Corridors” Constraint: Under Allied Four-Power agreements, commercial aircraft flying into Gatow had to strictly adhere to three 20-mile-wide Allied Air Corridors connecting Berlin to Frankfurt, Hanover, and Hamburg, flying below 10,000 feet to avoid Soviet radar interceptions.
  • Air France Interlude: Before Tegel opened to civilian jet traffic, Air France operated SNCASE Languedoc and DC-4 flights into Gatow alongside BEA to maintain the French sector’s civil access link.

3. The 1953 Baseline Year: Why Rebuild 1953?

By 1953, Gatow had evolved from a frantic, emergency Airlift hub into a highly organized, hardened Cold War gateway. The temporary PSP strips were replaced by dual parallel concrete runways, equipped with state-of-the-art Royal Air Force airfield lighting, early Instrument Landing Systems (ILS), and dedicated Non-Directional Beacons (NDB).

1. Georeferencing 1953 Archival Raster Data

Using high-resolution 1953 aerial survey imagery from public geospatial archives (GeoBasis-DE / LGB), we aligned Gatow’s exact historical footprint onto X-Plane 12’s global terrain mesh. This allowed us to strip away 21st-century residential developments and restore the original perimeter tree lines along the Havel.

2. Authentic 1953-Era RAF Markings

Instead of using modern ICAO standard markings, we hand-crafted the airfield’s ground geometry to match 1950s British Air Ministry specifications:

  • Vintage Threshold Markers: Custom-drawn early British-style threshold bars and runway numbers.
  • Dual Parallel Runway System: Mapped both the primary paved concrete vector and the secondary grass operational strips.
  • Dispersal Hardstands: Recreated the historic horseshoe-shaped dispersals used by RAF transport squadrons and visiting VIP flights.

3. Native Asset Optimization (100% Plug-and-Play)

To ensure maximum framerate performance, thermal efficiency, and zero missing-library errors (.obj crashes), the entire scenery is built strictly using optimized Laminar Research default 3D assets and PBR materials. You don't need to install dozens of third-party libraries—just drop the folder in and fly.

4. Reactivated Cold War Navigation (ILS & NDB)

We re-injected legacy navigation data directly into the airport’s layout framework:

  • Gatow NDB: Restored for NDB/DME procedural approaches out of the West German corridors.
  • Active ILS Approach: Fully aligned instrument guidance for low-visibility landings in marginal Berlin fog.

⚙️ Installation & Setup Guide

To ensure X-Plane 12 loads your restored 1953 Gatow layout over default global assets, follow this standard priority hierarchy:

  • Download and extract the project folder: RAF Gatow 1953 (or EDBG - RAF Gatow).
  • Move the folder into your X-Plane 12 Custom Scenery directory:
  • X-Plane 12/Custom Scenery/EDBG - RAF Gatow/
  • Launch X-Plane 12 once to generate/update scenery_packs.ini, then close the simulator.
  • Open Custom Scenery/scenery_packs.ini in any text editor and ensure EDBG - RAF Gatow sits ABOVE Global Scenery/Global Airports/:
SCENERY_PACK Custom Scenery/EDUQ - RAF Gatow/
...
SCENERY_PACK Global Scenery/Global Airports/
  • Launch X-Plane 12, select EDBG, and take off!
💡 Developer Note (WED): If you are editing this package inside WorldEditor, always export via File -> Export Scenery Pack. To clear cached DSF data in-sim without restarting, use Ctrl + R to reload scenery.
