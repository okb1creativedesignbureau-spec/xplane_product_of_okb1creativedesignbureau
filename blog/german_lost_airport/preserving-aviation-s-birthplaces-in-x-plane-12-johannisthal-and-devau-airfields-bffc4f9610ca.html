Preserving Aviation’s Birthplaces in X-Plane 12: Johannisthal and Devau Airfields

The landscape of modern aviation is built on the foundations of early 20th-century innovation. Long before long-haul jetliners and sprawling international hubs, the pioneering era of flight was defined by grass runways, wooden hangars, and experimental airframes.

In Europe, two airfields stand out as critical birthplaces of civil, commercial, and military aviation heritage: Flugplatz Johannisthal in Berlin and Devau Airport in Königsberg (now Kaliningrad).

As time, urban expansion, and wartime destruction have altered these historic sites, digital reconstruction in flight simulation offers a unique way to preserve their spatial legacy.

Flugplatz Johannisthal: The Cradle of German Aviation

Opened in 1909 as Germany’s first commercial airfield, Johannisthal located south of Berlin quickly became the epicenter of early European flight innovation and aviation culture.

  • The Birth of Air Contests: Johannisthal gained international fame hosting the Flugwoche (Flying Weeks), where aviation pioneers like Anthony Fokker, Melli Beese (Germany’s first licensed female pilot), and Hans Grade demonstrated early monoplanes and biplanes.
  • Industrial & Military Hub: During World War I, the airfield evolved into an industrial complex housing manufacturers like Albatros, Rumpler, and Fokker, playing a pivotal role in early military aviation design.
  • Early Airmail Operations: In 1919, Johannisthal served as a terminus for early German airmail services connecting Berlin to Weimar during the founding of the Weimar Republic.
  • Decline and Preservation: Commercial operations eventually shifted to Tempelhof in the 1920s. Johannisthal saw sporadic military and test use until its formal shutdown in 1952. Today, parts of the ground layout are protected as a heritage landscape and technology park.

Devau Airport: The World’s First Purpose-Built Passenger Terminal

Situated in East Prussia near historic Königsberg, Devau Airport holds a distinct title in civil aviation history: it was one of the world’s very first purpose-built commercial airports featuring a dedicated passenger terminal building.

  • Pioneering Commercial Architecture: Designed by architect Hanns Hopp and opened in 1919, Devau’s terminal set the functional template for modern passenger processing, blending ticket counters, waiting areas, and administration offices.
  • International Airlift Hub: In 1922, Devau became a vital stop on the Deruluft (German-Russian Air Transport Company) air route connecting Berlin and Moscow, establishing one of the earliest continuous international passenger routes across Europe.
  • Post-War Transition: Following the events of World War II and the regional boundary shifts, the airfield was absorbed into the Soviet network as Kaliningrad Devau, primarily serving utility aircraft, DOSAAF flight training, and parachuting clubs before operations ceased.

Historic Airfield Trivia

  • Fokker’s First Factory: Anthony Fokker established his early manufacturing operations at Johannisthal, building the famous Spinne (Spider) monoplane on-site.
  • First Female License in Germany: Melli Beese earned her pilot’s license at Johannisthal on her birthday in 1911, overcoming significant gender bias from contemporary male instructors.
  • Airmail Pioneers: The Berlin-Weimar airmail route originating out of Johannisthal in 1919 delivered national assembly documents, marking a key moment in post-WWI civil logistics.
  • International Air Gateway: Devau was among the first European airfields equipped with dedicated radio telegraphy infrastructure specifically intended for civil traffic navigation.
