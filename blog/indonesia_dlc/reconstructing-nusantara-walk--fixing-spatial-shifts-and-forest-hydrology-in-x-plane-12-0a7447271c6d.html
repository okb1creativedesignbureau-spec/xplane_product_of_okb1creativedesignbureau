Reconstructing Nusantara (WALK): Fixing Spatial Shifts and Forest Hydrology in X-Plane 12

How geospatial analysis, native WED tools, and hydrological alignment transformed a raw gateway placeholder into East Kalimantan’s modern VVIP capital hub.

If you take a low-level VFR flight into East Kalimantan using vanilla X-Plane 12 or early Gateway data, the aviation landscape tells an incomplete story. The newly constructed Nusantara International Airport (WALK) — the central VIP gateway serving Indonesia’s new capital (IKN) — either appears as an empty spatial field or a severely shifted, generic placeholder.

In reality, Nusantara Airport is an ambitious piece of civil engineering carved directly out of the Kalimantan rainforest. When desktop flight simulators isolate a runway from its surrounding tropical rivers, drainage canals, and regional road networks, they don’t just compromise visual fidelity — they fail to capture the operational context of flying in equatorial Borneo.

Here is how we overhauled WALK from the ground up using a first-principles geospatial workflow.

1. Correcting the Baseline: Spatial Realignment & Coordinate Calibration

Early or default gateway iterations of newly constructed airfields often suffer from significant spatial drift relative to actual Earth coordinates.

By re-referencing high-resolution satellite imagery against surveyed Earth coordinates, we shifted the entire airfield footprint back to its true geographic location. Runways, approach lighting paths, and terminal boundaries now line up precisely with real-world VFR reporting points and actual regional highway alignments leading into Penajam and the IKN core.

2. Radiometric Integrity: Fixing Apron & Taxiway Reflectance

One of the most noticeable flaws in default or rushed scenery uploads is the misuse of surface materials. Vanilla ground polygons often rely on default, highly reflective concrete or overly dark asphalt textures that fail under dynamic lighting.

  • Geospatial Reflectance Analysis: Using satellite imagery reflectance values, we re-mapped the taxiway and apron surfaces to match their real-world material composition.
  • Purging “Hallucinated” Infrastructure: Early auto-gen layouts often scatter generic assets — such as fictional sewage treatment facilities or misplaced cargo sheds — across the layout. We purged these hallucinated objects, replacing them with accurate terminal aprons, correct VVIP/VIP building colors, and the adjacent decorative water fountains.

3. Hydrological Integration: Canals, Meandering Rivers, & Rainforest Canopy

An airfield in East Kalimantan cannot be treated like an isolated concrete slab. The surrounding environment is defined by dense rainforest canopies, slow-moving tropical rivers, and specialized civil engineering drainage systems.

🛠️ Carving the Civil Canals:
During the physical construction of Nusantara Airport, engineers constructed massive perimeter drainage canals to manage heavy equatorial rainfall and prevent tarmac flooding. We manually vector-polygoned these engineering canals alongside the surrounding natural river bends, ensuring the airport transitions seamlessly into the local hydrological landscape.

4. Expanding the Operational Footprint: POLRI Helipad & Tactical VFR

To complete the operational environment of WALK, we expanded the scenery beyond the main 3,000-meter runway threshold:

  • Northern POLRI Helipad Integration: Situated just north of the main airfield perimeter sits the Indonesian National Police (POLRI) helipad compound. Although it operates as an uncontrolled VFR point in real-world operations, integrating its custom polygons, lighting, and landing pads gives rotary-wing pilots a realistic staging base right outside the primary VVIP barrier.
  • Highway & Infrastructure Weaving: The toll roads and primary access highways surrounding the airport were completely re-routed to match 2025/2026 infrastructure blueprints, bridging the gap between flight simulation and real-world urban development.

Technical Summary: WALK Overhaul

  • Location: Penajam North Paser Regency, East Kalimantan, Indonesia
  • ICAO Code: WALK
  • Core Improvements: Coordinate shift correction, calibrated PBR material reflectance, custom drainage canal vectors, accurate terminal color palette, and northern POLRI helipad integration.
  • Compatibility: 100% Native X-Plane 12 WorldEditor (WED) asset pipeline.

Building the Future of Indonesian Simulation

Whether you are flying high-altitude government transport sorties into the new capital or conducting low-level VFR helicopter runs along the Kalimantan coastline, this overhaul restores the true identity of IKN’s central hub.
